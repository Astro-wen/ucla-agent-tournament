AltruAgent AI Agent Gaming Tournament
Official Rules and Terms & Conditions
The AltruAgent AI Agent Gaming Tournament is a community competition in which 
participants build autonomous AI agents to compete in Pokémon Showdown, Red Alert, 
Honor of Kings, and Werewolf.
The tournament celebrates agent design, reasoning, strategy, cooperation, and fair 
competition. People build the agents; the agents play the games.
The essential rules are:
•
Autonomous play: Agents must make and submit gameplay decisions without 
human control, coaching, approval, or other intervention during competitive play.
•
Player-view information only: Agents may use only information permitted for their 
assigned player or role. Livestreams, spectator views, hidden game state, and 
unauthorized information about opponents are off limits.
•
Fair competition: No collusion, match fixing, unauthorized access, interference with 
other agents, or exploitation of platform vulnerabilities.
•
Accountability: Each entry must provide a public source repository and identify the 
version used in competition. 
Agents and repositories may be edited until 11:59 p.m. 
Pacific Time on October 13, 2026; after that, no further edits may be made to a 
submitted agent’s repository. 
Participants must reasonably cooperate with 
compliance checks.
•
Tournament results: Depending on the game and announced format, winners will be 
determined by points earned or advancement through elimination rounds.
•
Prizes: Each of the four games receives 25% of the distributable prize pool. Within 
each game, first place receives 50%, second place 30%, and third place 20% of that 
game’s allocation. Matcherino will cover fees, publish the distributable pool, and 
distribute prizes.
To submit an entry or win a prize, a participant must be eligible to 
hold a Matcherinoaccount and receive payouts through it (see Section 2).
This summary does not replace the detailed rules below.
1. Acceptance and scope
These Official Rules and Terms & Conditions (“Rules”) govern registration, agent 
submissions, competitive matches, tournament-related conduct, and prize eligibility.

4. Autonomous agents and human involvement
Agents must operate autonomously during official competitive play. This includes any in-match drafting, team selection, role actions, discussion, voting, and other decisions 
affecting a match.
Participants may design, train, test, and configure their agents before the 
Submission 
Deadline
.
After it, participants may continue testing their agents as long asthey do not 
change the submitted version.
Unless game-specific rules say otherwise, agents may 
combine model-based reasoning, planning, search, and programmed strategies.
Once a match begins, no person may directly or indirectly:
•
Select, enter, approve, reject, edit, or replace gameplay actions or messages.
•
Provide strategic advice, prompts, hints, evaluations, or recommendations.
•
Change the agent’s code, model selection, prompts, configuration, tools, or 
decision-making memory.
•
Manually choose between proposed model outputs or agent strategies.
•
Supply information obtained from watching the match or another source.
•
Substitute human decisions for agent decisions through MCP or any other interface.
These restrictions apply even if the assistance does not improve performance or change 
the result. Routing human decisions through an automated system does not make them 
autonomous.
Preconfigured automatic retries,
reconnections, authentication refreshes, and fault 
recovery are permitted if they preserve the submitted agent’s behavior and do not 
introduce prohibited information.

5. Permitted information and external services
During a match, an agent may use:
•
Information delivered through the authorized player-facing interface for its own seat 
or role.
•
Messages received through communication channels permitted for that game.
•
Its own calculations and memory derived from permitted information within that 
match.
•
Pre-existing model knowledge, submitted code, and fixed reference materials 
permitted by the game-specific rules.
External model inference and hosting services are allowed unless the game-specific rules 
restrict them. They must process only permitted inputs and must not supply live 
competitive information, human assistance, or unauthorized communication.
Agents must not access or use:
•
Livestreams, recordings of an ongoing match, commentary, spectator chat, or 
spectator dashboards.
•
Live leaderboards or other participants’ results that are not expressly available 
through the authorized player interface.
•
Another player’s private observations, unrevealed roles, pending actions, private 
messages, or decision-making state.
•
Opponents’ runtime logs, private files, credentials, or internal prompts.
•
Administrative interfaces, unauthorized endpoints, or game-server internals.
•
Internet searches, remote data feeds, shared files, or other channels used to obtain 
information about an ongoing match or opponent outside the permitted player view.
These restrictions apply to the entire agent system, including helper agents, tools, shared 
memory, remote services, and infrastructure. Information may not be passed through an 
intermediary to evade a restriction.

Legitimate deductions from permitted observations are allowed. The prohibition concerns 
unauthorized access to information, not an agent’s ability to infer what may be happening.
Each match must start with fresh match-specific memory unless the published game-specific rules expressly permit persistent memory or learning across matches. Concurrent 
matches must remain isolated. Frozen code, model weights, and permitted static 
reference materials may be reused.
If an agent unexpectedly receives information it should not have, the participant must 
promptly report the issue and must not knowingly continue exploiting it.
6. Spectating and livestreams
Matches may be livestreamed, recorded, commentated on, and made available as replays. 
Spectator views may reveal information unavailable to individual players.
Participants may watch tournament coverage only when doing so cannot feed information 
into or otherwise influence an active entry. A person authorized to perform technical 
recovery for an active entry must not simultaneously monitor that entry’s match through a 
spectator view.
Spectators, collaborators, and other third parties must not relay prohibited information or 
instructions to an active agent or its supporting systems. Participants must not request, 
arrange, or knowingly accept such assistance.
A delayed broadcast is still an unauthorized information source for an active agent. 
Elimination from a match does not permit a participant or their agent to assist players who 
remain active.
Organizers may delay broadcasts, withhold sensitive information, or restrict spectator 
access to protect competitive integrity.
7. Fair play, communication, and conduct
Participants and their agents must compete in good faith under the objectives of the 
applicable game.
Prohibited conduct includes:
•
Match fixing, bribery, deliberately arranging outcomes, or manipulating rankings.
•
Undisclosed duplicate entries or coordinated entries used to benefit one another.
•
Sharing hidden information across opposing entries or communicating outside 
permitted game channels.

•
Impersonating another participant, an Organizer, or an official system message.
•
Accessing another entry’s account, credentials, files, or infrastructure without 
authorization.
•
Circumventing access controls, rate limits, resource limits, or information 
restrictions.
•
Disrupting servers or agents, including denial-of-service behavior, malicious 
payloads, or deliberately abusive requests.
•
Knowingly exploiting a bug to obtain unauthorized information, actions, or 
competitive advantages.
•
Falsifying eligibility, repository contents, deployment records, or audit evidence.
Ordinary in-game strategy remains permitted. This includes bluffing, deception about 
game roles or intentions, alliances, and cooperation where the game allows them. Those 
activities must occur through permitted game mechanisms and pursue legitimate in-game 
objectives.
In-game messages may not be used to induce an opponent’s system to reveal credentials, 
access unrelated files, execute unauthorized code, or bypass tournament restrictions. 
Persuasion about game decisions is permitted; attacks on the opponent’s underlying 
system are not.
Participants must report suspected vulnerabilities privately to Organizers and must not 
exploit them while awaiting a ruling.
Harassment, threats, discriminatory abuse, doxxing, and publication of another person’s 
private information are prohibited in tournament-related spaces. Participants must take 
reasonable steps to prevent their agents from generating such content and cooperate with 
corrective measures.

9. Verification and audits

Organizers may conduct reasonable compliance checks before, during, or after the 
tournament, including random checks, finalist verification, and investigations of reported 
violations.
Participants must retain reasonable records sufficientto connect their submitted version 
to competitive play. These should include the deployed commit and configuration, relevant 
MCP requests and responses, action timestamps, model/service identifiers, and records of 
any 
manual interventions.
Records must be preserved for the period described in Section 8. Participants are not 
required to produce inaccessible proprietary model internals.
Organizers may request relevant source code, configuration records, logs, dependency 
details, a demonstration, or a supervised rerun. Where reasonably necessary, Organizers 
may request a limited walkthrough of the machine or hosted environment used to run 
the 
agent.
Review must be proportionate to the compliance question. Participants should redact 
unrelated personal information and must not disclose passwords, API keys, or other 
secrets. A supervised demonstration or other reasonable alternative may be used where 
unrestricted access would expose unrelated or protected information.
Organizers may compare repository history, platform records, participant-provided 
evidence, and observed behavior. Differences caused by model nondeterminism or 
provider updates are not automatically evidence of misconduct, but participants must 
explain relevant discrepancies honestly.
Failure to preserve required records, materially misleading submissions, destruction of 
relevant evidence, or refusal to reasonably cooperate may independently result in 
disqualification or loss of prize eligibility.
Nonpublic audit materials will be used for tournament administration, integrity review, 
dispute handling, and legal compliance, with access limited to people who reasonably 
need it.
10. Technical failures and match administration
Participants are responsible for preparing their agents, maintaining required services, and 
meeting published check-in and action deadlines.
A participant-side crash, lost connection, exhausted API credit, or provider outage does not 
automatically entitle an entry to a replay. Published timeout and forfeit rules apply unless 
Organizers authorize another remedy.

For platform failures, security incidents, or other material irregularities, Organizers may 
pause, resume, replay, reschedule, or void affected matches. They will consider reliable 
game records, the effect on the result, and fairness to all affected entries.
Participants must not intentionally disconnect, stall, or create failures to obtain a replay or 
other advantage.
11. Results, violations, and appeals
Platform scores and announced placements remain provisional until applicable 
verification and timely disputes are resolved.
Organizers may investigate suspected violations and temporarily suspend an entry or 
withhold certification of its results while reviewing the matter.
Available remedies include a warning, required correction, loss of points, match forfeiture, 
removal from a leaderboard or bracket, disqualification, prize ineligibility, and exclusion 
from future Organizer-run events.
Sanctions will reflect the seriousness of the conduct, its competitive effect, intent, 
repetition, and cooperation with the investigation. A violation may be sanctioned without 
proof that it changed the outcome.
Before a final disqualification or prize forfeiture, Organizers will ordinarily explain the 
material concern and give the participant a reasonable opportunity to respond. Immediate 
protective action may be taken when needed to protect the event or its systems.
A participant may request one review within 48 hours of receiving a decision by contacting 
Organizers and identifying the alleged error or new evidence. Where practical, a person not 
responsible for the initial decision will participate in the review.
Organizers’ decisions following review are final for tournament administration, without 
limiting rights that cannot lawfully be waived.
